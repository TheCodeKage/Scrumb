AI SPRINT PLANNING

Scrumb replaces gut-feel sprint planning with an AI-generated dependency graph — stack locked, tasks assigned by skill, critical path calculated, scope trimmed automatically when the deadline math breaks.

THE PROBLEM

WHY SPRINT PLANNING FAILS

Most sprint planning sessions end with teams committing to more than they can build, on timelines they can't meet, with tasks assigned based on who spoke up first rather than who has the right skills. The plan looks fine in the meeting. It falls apart by day three.

The root cause is almost always the same: planning without architecture. A list of tasks with no dependency information and no skill-to-owner matching is just a to-do list with a due date.

THE ARCHITECT AUDIT

HOW SCRUMB PLANS SPRINTS

Scrumb's sprint planning starts with the Architect Audit — a technical MCQ interview that locks in your stack, team skills, and MVP scope before any tasks are created.

The audit prevents the three most common sprint planning failures:

  • Stack ambiguity: "We'll use whatever makes sense" becomes a concrete technology choice
  • Scope inflation: Feature creep is bounded before it becomes a task
  • Skill mismatch: Tasks are assigned to people who can actually do them
THE OUTPUT

WHAT YOU GET: A TASK DAG, NOT A LIST

After the audit, Scrumb generates a Directed Acyclic Graph of sprint tasks. Every task has a parent, an owner, an estimate, and a set of dependencies. The critical path — where any delay cascades to everything downstream — is calculated and highlighted automatically.

// SPRINT DAG FRAGMENT
[ setup/db-schema ] @Priya · 2hrs · START HERE
  └→ [ backend/api/endpoints ] @Rohan · 4hrs · BLOCKED
      └→ [ frontend/dashboard ] @Aditya · 3hrs · BLOCKED

CRITICAL PATH: 9hrs. Any slip here cascades to deadline.
PANIC MODE

WHEN THE PLAN BREAKS DOWN

Sprint planning is not a one-time event. Scrumb continuously monitors velocity against the deadline. When the math stops working, Panic Mode activates automatically — analyzing the remaining task tree, identifying which tasks are critical for a working demo, and archiving everything else.

This is automated scope management. The plan adapts in real time so the team can ship something, even if it's smaller than originally planned.
